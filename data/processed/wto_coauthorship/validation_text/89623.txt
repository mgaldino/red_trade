              JOINT PROPOSAL ON THE IMPROVEMENT OF DIVERSIFICATION
                             OF THE WTO SECRETARIAT

                    By Brazil, China, Cuba, Ecuador, India, Pakistan, South Africa


1.       We appreciate the efforts by the WTO Secretariat to improve the diversification of its
composition. However, based on the statistics provided by the Secretariat on its website
(http://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/secre_e/intro_e.htm) by October 9, 2009, diversification
obviously remains a serious systemic issue. Nationals from developing Members, which constitute
more than two thirds of WTO Membership, amount to only one fifth of the total Secretariat staff.
And nationals from the top eight Members, those with most nationals in the total Secretariat staff,
amount to nearly 70 per cent and they are all developed ones.

2.       While recognizing that merits should be one, but not the only one, of the key elements to
consider applications, we believe that diversification must remain a long-term and key element in the
WTO secretariat recruitment policy and concrete measures must be taken to constantly improve the
situation. Relevant practices of other international organizations will provide important experiences
for us to share:

        (a)      Under the item of “Nationalities” of the recent Vacancy Announcement D2013 of
                 WIPO published on 16th November 2009, it is clearly indicated that: “Other
                 qualifications being equal, preference is given to candidate who are nationals of one
                 of the Member States of WIPO and the candidates from regions of the world from
                 which the number of WIPO staff is insufficient.”

        (b)      The Statement of Diversity of IMF on January 4, 2008 pointed out that: “An IMF
                 staff that reflects the diversity of its membership will enhance legitimacy and
                 understanding of the Fund’s role, and strengthen the Fund’s ability to serve its
                 member countries.” (http://www.imf.org/external/hrd/diversity.htm).

        (c)      The recruitment policy of the United Nations University reads: “While recruiting
                 only the most able and technically competent candidates, we strive for balance in
                 gender and nationality representation. We do not set country or gender quotas, but
                 we do try to ensure that the diversity of UN member states is reflected in the
                 composition of our staff. Where competing candidates exhibit equal merit, preference
                 is given to candidates from developing countries or regions and every effort is made
                 to achieve a balanced gender distribution within the organisation.”
                 (http://www.inweh.unu.edu/vacancies.htm)

        (d)      The Diversity & Inclusion of the World Bank Group reads: “Diversity & Inclusion
                 are at the heart of how we define excellence…Our diversity is our comparative
                 advantage as a global knowledge and development organization…We do not just
                 recognize our diversity; through inclusion, we mobilize it. We are working to create
                 an inclusive and respectful work environment where we demonstrate that we fully

3.      We urge the Committee on Budget, Finance and Administration (CBFA) to initiate a
permanent process of discussion on the issue of diversification in various formats. To facilitate such
discussion, we suggest that the following measures be taken:

        (a)     To instruct the Secretariat to prepare a thorough evaluation of the diversification issue
                and report to the CBFA by March 1st 2010. Such report should also include
                recommendations on how to improve the situation;

        (b)     To include diversification as a permanent item on the agenda of the CBFA for formal
                discussions complemented by other forms of consultation;

        (c)     To instruct the Secretariat to report separately on the diversification issue annually at
                the last CBFA meeting, including information on the state of play, measures taken
                and effects;

        (d)     To report on the diversification issue to the General Council annually at its last
                meeting, including recommendations for the General Council to take necessary
                actions.

4.      Finally, this joint proposal remain open for all other interested Members to co-sponsor.
