Article 14 – (Topic 1 from Annex I to the Chairman’s paper circulated by fax on
17 December 2001)

Issue3 1 – In principle, we favour greater transparency/predictability in the application of the
Agreement. Is Brazil seeking further elaboration of paragraphs (a) to (d) of Article 14 or does Brazil
perceive deficiencies in coverage beyond current Article 14. If so, what would those deficiencies be?

Issue 2 – Is Brazil’s intent simply to codify existing WTO case law (Canada-Regional Aircraft)
which has held that Article 1.1(b) of the ASCM does not admit of a “cost to government” approach
to determining benefit?

Issue 3 – Is India suggesting that, where the specified financial contribution (i.e., the forgoing or non-
collection of duties) occurs after the period of investigation, there is no benefit notwithstanding that
the eligibility requirements were satisfied and legal entitlement to the financial contribution vested
during the investigation period? Is a benefit in terms of the marketplace not conferred at the time
legal entitlement to a financial contribution vests in the recipient (insofar as the financial contribution
becomes a receivable of the enterprise concerned, which can be factored into its product pricing
equation)?

Issue 4 – What does India perceive to be the deficiencies in current footnote 1 and items (h) and (i) of
Annex 1? Given that the remission or drawback of duties otherwise owing are, by their very nature,
financial contributions, is it not incumbent on granting Members to ensure that their programmes are
administered in a manner that allows for verification of the amounts by which exemptions or
remissions exceed duties which have accrued?

De minimis –(Topic 3 from Annex I to the Chairman’s paper circulated by fax on
17 December 2001)

Issue 1 – While the relevancy of de minimis is clear in investigations, its application in the
enforcement stage is less so. In particular, where the amount of subsidy was not found to be de
minimis in the original investigation and resulting injury to the domestic industry was established, the
levying of countervailing duties on all subject imports, (including particular shipments benefiting
from de minimis amounts of subsidy), may be justified both for valid policy and administrative
reasons.
