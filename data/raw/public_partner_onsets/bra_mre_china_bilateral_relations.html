People’s Republic of China

Share by Facebook Share by Twitter Share by LinkedIn Share by WhatsApp link to Copy to Clipboard
Published in Jan 01, 2016 07:58 PM Updated in May 06, 2026 05:17 PM

Brazil-China relations were established in 1974, when the Embassy of Brazil in Beijing and the Embassy of China in Brasília were opened. Brazil has Consulates General in Shanghai, Guangzhou, Hong Kong and Chengdu. China has Consulates General in Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo and Recife.

Bilateral relations have showed a significant dynamism. Since 2009, China has been Brazil's major trading partner and one of the main sources of foreign investment flows into the country. Relations between the two countries go beyond the bilateral arena: Brazil and China have also held talks in mechanisms such as  BRICS, G20, WTO and BASIC (a coordinating bloc consisting of Brazil, South Africa, India and China to discuss environmental issues).

In 1993, Brazil and China established a "Strategic Partnership". Bilateral relations were elevated to a “Global Strategic Partnership” in 2012, and to a “Brazil–China Community with a Shared Future for a More Just World and a More Sustainable Planet” in 2024.

In 2004, COSBAN was established as the highest permanent body for bilateral dialogue and cooperation, co-chaired by the Vice Presidents of Brazil and China. Its plenary sessions were held in Beijing (2006), Brasília (2012), Guangzhou (2013), Brasília (2015), Beijing (2019), and Brasília (May 2022, in virtual format). The 7th Plenary Session of COSBAN took place in Beijing on June 6, 2024, co-chaired by Vice President Alckmin and Vice President Han Zheng.

The Global Strategic Dialogue (GSD), established in 2012, is also noteworthy. It is a structured dialogue mechanism at the level of Ministers of Foreign Affairs, aimed at exchanging views on issues related to the bilateral, regional, and multilateral agendas. The instrument reflects the global dimension of the bilateral relationship, broadening the scope of discussions and identifying areas of common interest, including topics on the international agenda.

The fourth and most recent edition of the GSD was held in January 2024 in Brasília, co-chaired by the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Ambassador Mauro Vieira, and the Minister of Foreign Affairs of the People’s Republic of China, Wang Yi. The previous editions of the GSD took place in Brasília in April 2014; in Beijing in June 2017; and in Brasília in July 2019.

In April 2023, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva made his third State visit to China. The visit resulted in the most comprehensive joint statement ever issued by Brazil and China, demonstrating the multidimensional nature of their shared interests. Fifteen governmental documents were signed and 32 business agreements were announced in areas such as renewable energies; industry; automotive; agribusiness; green lines of credit; information technology; health; and infrastructure. In addition, nine ageements  were signed between Brazilian states and other Brazilian and Chinese agencies and companies. In the environmental field, a joint declaration was adopted on the fight against climate change and a specific subcommittee on the theme was created within the scope of COSBAN.

Vice President Geraldo Alckmin, in turn, traveled to China in June 2024, a year that marked the 50th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations and the 20th anniversary of the creation of COSBAN. On that occasion, eight intergovernmental instruments were signed and thirty outcomes were announced, in addition to eleven private-sector agreements, covering areas such as agriculture, finance, environment and climate change, trade, industry, communications, health, education, culture, outer space, energy, micro and small enterprises, social and rural development, and science, technology, and innovation.

In November 2024, President Xi Jinping paid a State Visit to Brazil, marking the high point of the 50th anniversary celebrations of diplomatic relations. In addition to elevating the relationship to the level of a “Community with a Shared Future for a More Just World and a More Sustainable Planet,” 37 new cooperation instruments were signed during the visit, in areas such as agriculture, trade, communications, education, energy, industry, investment, health, and tourism. Possible synergies between the two countries’ investment policies and programs were also discussed.

In May 2025, President Lula paid another State Visit to China, the second during his current term. He was accompanied by a large delegation of representatives from the Legislative and Executive branches, as well as from the private sector. A total of 36 new bilateral instruments or projects were signed or announced, covering areas such as trade, investment, finance, science and technology, artificial intelligence, agriculture and livestock, family farming, the digital economy, mining, sustainable development, space, communications, reforestation, intellectual property, and health. At a business forum held on the sidelines of the visit, commercial agreements were announced in sectors such as sustainable aviation fuel, green hydrogen and ammonia, mining, semiconductor production, food and delivery services, and pharmaceuticals.

Brazil–China trade flows have expanded significantly. China has been Brazil’s largest trading partner since 2009. From just over US$3 billion in the early 2000s, bilateral trade has grown many times over, reaching a record US$170.9 billion in 2025—for the tenth consecutive year at its highest level in history. Of this total, US$100 billion account for  Brazilian exports to China.

China is among the main sources of foreign direct investment in Brazil, with a growing presence in key sectors of the national economy. Investment is particularly strong in electricity, manufacturing (including automobiles), information technology, oil extraction, and agriculture.

Financial cooperation has also intensified. Notable initiatives include the Brazil–China Cooperation Fund for the Expansion of Productive Capacity for Sustainable Development, relaunched in 2024 to facilitate the financing of investment projects, as well as progress in local-currency trade and stock exchange connectivity, including the launch of Brazilian index funds in China and Chinese index funds in Brazil.

China is one of Brazil’s key partners in space cooperation, through collaboration between the Brazilian Space Agency (AEB) and, on the Chinese side, the China National Space Administration (CNSA). This partnership dates back to 1988, when the founding agreement of the China-Brazil Earth Resources Satellite (CBERS) program was signed, aimed at the joint development of Earth observation satellites. Six jointly developed satellites have already been launched, two of which remain operational (CBERS-4 and CBERS-4A).

In 2023, during President Lula’s State Visit to China, a protocol was signed for the development and launch of the CBERS-6 satellite, the first in the bilateral program to feature radar sensing, as well as the China–Brazil Space Cooperation Plan 2023–2032. In turn, Brazil’s Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation (MCTI) and the China National Space Administration (CNSA) concluded in 2026 the signing of the Complementary Protocol on the Joint Development of CBERS-5, which will provide Brazil with greater autonomy in meteorological data collection.

Also of note are activities under the China–Brazil Joint Laboratory for Space Weather and bilateral cooperation in the BINGO (Baryon Acoustic Oscillations from Integrated Neutron Gas Observations) radio telescope project, the largest international scientific cooperation project led by Brazilian researchers in the space field.

Chronology of bilateral relations

1974 – Establishment of diplomatic relations (August)

1975 – Inauguration of the Chinese Embassy in Brasília

1976 – Inauguration of the Brazilian Embassy in Beijing

1977 – The President of the Foreign Trade Foundation, Marcus Vinicius Pratini de Moraes, visits China 

1977 – Participation of a Chinese delegation in Brasil Export 77, in São Paulo

1978 – Signing of the Trade Agreement between Brazil and China

1979 – The Vice Premier of the State Council of China, Kang Shien, visits Brazil 

1980 – 1st Meeting of the Brazil–People’s Republic of China Joint Trade Commission

1980 – Visit of seven Chinese business missions to Brazil

1982 – Minister of Foreign Affairs Ramiro Saraiva Guerreiro visits China (March)

1982 – Signing of the Scientific and Technological Cooperation Agreement.

1982 – 2nd Meeting of the Brazil–People’s Republic of China Joint Trade Commission.

1982 – Inauguration in Beijing of representative offices of Banco do Brasil, Braspetro, and Brasilinvest.

1982 – Beginning of exports of Comexport products to China.

1984 – President João Baptista Figueiredo visits China (May).

1984 – Foreign Minister Wu Xueqian visits Brazil (August).

1985 – Establishment of the Chinese Consulate General in São Paulo.

1985 – Premier Zhao Ziyang visits Brazil (November).

1988 – President José Sarney visits China (July).

1988 – The Brazil-China Earth Resources Satellite Program (CBERS) is established (July)

1990 – President Yang Shangkun visits Brazil (May)

1992 – Premier Li Peng visits Brazil (June)

1992 – Inauguration of the Consulate General of the People’s Republic of China in Rio de Janeiro.

1993 – State Councillor and Minister of Foreign Affairs Qian Qichen visits Brazil (March)

1993 – Premier Zhou Rongji visits Brazil. Establishment of the Brazil-China Strategic Partnership (May/June)

1993 – Creation of the first Brazil–China Parliamentary Group in the Chamber of Deputies (August).

1993 – President Jiang Zemin visits Brazil (November)

1994 – Reopening of the Consulate General in Shanghai.

1994 – Inauguration of the Consulate General in Hong Kong.

1995 – President Fernando Henrique Cardoso visits China (December)

1995 – Brazil announces its support for China’s accession to the World Trade Organization (WTO)

1996 – Premier Li Peng visits Brazil (November)

1998 – Minister of Foreign Affairs Luiz Felipe Lampreia visits China (November)

1999 – Launching of the CBERS-1 satellite (October)

1999 – Vice President Marco Maciel visits China (December)

2000 – Foreign Minister Tang Jiaxuan visits Brazil (September)

2000 – China  becomes Brazil’s  largest trading partner in Asia

2001 – President Jiang Zemin visits Brazil (April)

2001 – China joins the World Trade Organization (11 December).

2003 – Launching of the CBERS-2 satellite (October)

2004 – Minister of Foreign Affairs Celso Amorim visits China (February)

2004 – President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva visits China  (May)

2004 –  The Brazil-China High-Level Commission for Coordination and Cooperation (COSBAN) is established (May)

2004 – Establishment of the first China–Brazil Parliamentary Group in the Federal Senate (May)

2004 – President Hu Jintao visits Brazil.

2006 – The First COSBAN meeting is held in Beijing, chaired by Vice President José Alencar on the Brazilian side and by Deputy Prime-Minister Mme Wu Yi on the Chinese side (March)

2006 – The Chairman of China’s National People’s Congress, Wu Bangguo, visits Brazil (August)

2007 – The Strategic Dialogue between Foreign Ministries is established (April)

2007 – Tthe CBERS-2B satellite is launched (September)

2007 – The first Brazil-China Strategic Dialogue Meeting is held in Beijing (November)

2008 – He Guoqiang, member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China, visits Brazil  (July)

2008 – President Lula da Silva visits China and attends the opening ceremony of the Beijing Olympics (August)

2009 – Foreign Minister Yang Jiechi visits Brazil (January)

2009 – Vice President Xi Jinping visits Brazil  (February)

2009 – President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva makes a state visit to China (May 19)

2009 – Hao Chiyong, Deputy Minister of the Supreme People's Court, visits Brazil (May)

2009 – First BRIC Leaders’ Meeting in Yekaterinburg, Russia (June).

2009 – The President of the Supreme Court, Gilmar Mendes, visits China (September)

2009 – The Chairman of the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC), Jia Qinglin, visits Brazil (November)

2009 – First coordinated action of the BASIC group (Brazil, South Africa, India, China) at COP-15 in Copenhagen (December).

2009 – China becomes Brazil's main trading partner

2009 – Opening of the Brazilian Trade and Investment Promotion Agency (ApexBrasil) office in Beijing.

2010 – President Hu Jintao pays a State visit to Brazil and takes part in the 2nd BRICS Summit in Brasília. Signing of the Brazil-China Joint Action Plan 2010-2014 (April)

2010 – The Head Minister of the Institutional Security Cabinet, General Jorge Félix, visits China (April)

2011 – The Minister of Commerce of the People’s Republic of China, Chen Deming, visits Brazil (May 14-17)

2011 – Ministerial Meeting of Brazil, South Africa, India and China (BASIC) on Climate Change (Inhotim - MG, August 26-27)

2011 – Signing of the Brazil-China Joint Health Action Plan 2011-2014 (October)

2011 – President Dilma Rousseff and President Hu Jintao meet on the sidelines of the 6th G20 Summit, in Cannes, France  (December)

2011 – Opening of the Brazilian Consulate General in Guangzhou.

2012 – Chinese Premier Wen Jiabao visits Brazil and takes part in the Rio+20 Earth Summit, in Rio de Janeiro. Signing of the 2012-2021 10-Year Cooperation Plan. Bilateral relations are elevated to "Global Strategic Partnership" and the Foreign Ministers Global Strategic Dialogue is established (Rio de Janeiro, June 21)  

2012 – Presidents Dilma Rousseff and Hu Jintao meet on the sidelines of the 7th G20 Summit, in Los Cabos, Mexico (June)

2012 – The President of the Chamber of Deputies, Deputy Marco Maia, visits China (June)

2012 – China becomes the main importer of Brazilian products

2013 – 3rd Meeting of Brazil-China Consultations on Migration and Consular Issues (Brasília, May 22)

2013 – Member of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China and Secretary of the Beijing Municipal Committee of the Party, Guo Jinlong, visits Brazil (June)

2013 – Mission of the Minister Chief of the Civil Staff of the Presidency of Brazil, Gleisi Hoffmann, and of the Minister of Development, Industry, and Foreign Trade, Fernando Pimentel, to China (August)

2013 – The 16th Ministerial Meeting of Brazil, South Africa, India and China (BASIC) on Climate Change is held in Foz do Iguaçu ( September 15-16)

2013 – President Dilma Rousseff with and the President Xi Jinping meet on the sidelines of  the 8th G20 Summit in Saint Petersburg, Russia (September)

2013 – Month of Brazil in China (September) and of China in Brazil (October)

2013 – Vice President Michel Temer makes an official visit to China: he  participates in the opening ceremony of the 4th Ministerial Conference of the Macau Forum and in the third COSBAN Meeting, in Guangzhou, and meets with President Xi Jinping and Vice President Li Yuanchao (November 4-9)

2013 – The  CBERS-3 satellite is launched (December)
