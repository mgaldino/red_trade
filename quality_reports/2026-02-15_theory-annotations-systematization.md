## PART I: Individual Annotations (Summary)

### Paper 1: Malis (2021) — *International Organization*
**"Conflict, Cooperation, and Delegated Diplomacy"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | 3 subsections: The effects of ambassadorial turnover (~2pp) → Diplomats as agents (~3pp) → Continuity during turnover (~1pp). Argument-first, literature woven in. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "diplomatic representation quality." Manipulation = ambassadorial rotation (turnover events). Bridge: principal-agent framework (ambassadors as agents who reduce information asymmetry and increase cooperation). |
| **Hypotheses** | 3 formal propositions: H1 (turnover → increased conflict), H2 (turnover → decreased cooperation), H3 (turnover → increased trade barriers). Derived from agency theory applied to diplomacy. |
| **Mechanism** | Agency theory: ambassadors provide private information about partner preferences, invest in relationships, and facilitate bargaining. Turnover disrupts this, increasing coordination failures. Three channels: information loss, relationship capital destruction, signaling disruption. |
| **Theory→Results** | H1-H3 tested directly with panel data exploiting ambassadorial rotation as quasi-random variation. "Continuity during turnover" subsection explicitly bounds what the theory does NOT predict. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Scope-limiting as strength"**: The subsection on what should NOT change (ongoing wars, trade agreements) steelmans objections and shows theoretical precision. |

### Paper 2: Carnegie & Marinov (2017) — *AJPS*
**"Foreign Aid, Human Rights, and Democracy Promotion"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Multi-subsection institutional chain: Council → Presidency → Commission → Recipient. Each subsection adds one link, simultaneously building theory AND establishing identification. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "EU aid with conditionality." Manipulation = rotating EU Council presidency (former colonizers budget more aid for former colonies during presidency). |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal H1/H2/H3. Embedded expectations: (1) presidency → more aid to former colonies, (2) aid + conditionality → reforms, (3) effects are short-lived. |
| **Mechanism** | Three-actor institutional chain: Council presidency → aid commitments → Commission conditionality → recipient reforms. Positive conditionality (carrots). |
| **Theory→Results** | First-stage tests expectation 1, second-stage (IV) tests reform effects, temporal patterns match short-lived prediction. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Institutional-chain-as-theory"**: The theory IS the identification strategy. Understanding one requires understanding the other. |

### Paper 3: Flores-Macias & Kreps (2013) — *JOP*
**"The Foreign Policy Consequences of Trade"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Hirschmanian framework: trade → vested interests → political alignment. Standard application of existing theory to new context. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "commercial dependence effects on alignment." Manipulation = bilateral trade volumes (instrumented via 2SLS). Weak identification — instrument's exclusion restriction contested. |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses. Core prediction: greater trade with China → greater UNGA convergence with China. |
| **Mechanism** | Hirschman's vested interests: trade creates domestic constituencies who benefit from good relations with China, pushing for alignment. |
| **Theory→Results** | OLS → 2SLS progression. Mechanism (vested interests) is theoretical but unobserved. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Existing theory + new context"**: Contribution is empirical (first systematic evidence), not theoretical. |

### Paper 4: Kuziemko & Werker (2006) — *JPE*
**"How Much Is a Seat on the Security Council Worth?"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Introduction states 3 competing explanations, then "Political Economy of the UNSC" section provides institutional detail + congressional analogy. Minimal formal theory. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "vote-buying at the UN." Manipulation = discontinuous UNSC membership (fixed 2-year terms, no reelection) + interaction with world events. |
| **Hypotheses** | "Three reasons" framing (not formal H labels): (1) bribery/vote-buying, (2) increased salience, (3) omitted variable (integration). Structured for empirical elimination. |
| **Mechanism** | Vote-buying: UNSC membership → temporary leverage → great powers increase aid → compliance on votes. Time-limited exchange. |
| **Theory→Results** | Hypothesis elimination: interaction with "important years" favors bribery over salience; temporal pattern (sharp rise/fall) favors bribery over integration; UNICEF decomposition identifies US-controlled channel. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Competing hypotheses for empirical elimination"**: Theory is a set of alternatives; contribution is the design that discriminates among them. |

### Paper 5: Dreher, Fuchs, Parks, Strange & Tierney (2018) — *ISQ*
**"Apples and Dragon Fruits"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | 3 subsections (Foreign Policy Interests, Economic Interests, Governance), each ending with a formal labeled hypothesis. Most formally structured of all 15 papers. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Not a causal identification paper. Studies determinants of Chinese financing (ODA vs. OOF) — correlational, not causal. |
| **Hypotheses** | 3 formal, labeled, directional hypotheses (H1, H2, H3). Each maps to the ODA/OOF disaggregation. |
| **Mechanism** | Classification theory: ODA serves political goals (vested interests, One-China), OOF serves commercial goals (trade, resources, repayment). Different flow types = different allocation logics. |
| **Theory→Results** | Direct mapping: 5-column regression tables (total, ODA, OOF, grants, loans). Each hypothesis tested by comparing coefficients across columns. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Conceptual disaggregation as theory"**: The primary theoretical move is taxonomic (ODA ≠ OOF). Hypotheses are clear, directional, and well-connected to empirical design. |

### Paper 6: Dube, Dube & Garcia-Ponce (2013) — *APSR*
**"Cross-Border Spillover: U.S. Gun Laws and Violence in Mexico"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | "Background" section (not labeled "theory") covers: institutional context → FAWB expiration → supply chain mechanics → prior literature. Theory is implicit in the institutional description. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "gun availability." Manipulation = FAWB expiration (Sept 2004) creating a natural experiment. Bridge via supply-chain proximity: Mexican municipalities closer to Texas ports of entry are more exposed. |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses. Implicit predictions: FAWB expiration → more assault weapons flowing south → more homicides in proximate Mexican municipalities. |
| **Mechanism** | Supply-chain/logistics: assault weapons previously banned become available → flow through Texas gun dealers → cross US-Mexico border at entry ports → increase violence in nearby Mexican municipalities. |
| **Theory→Results** | DiD exploiting FAWB expiration, with distance-to-port-of-entry as treatment intensity. Supply chain mechanism confirmed via gun seizure data and weapon-type analysis. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Institutional context as implicit theory"**: No formal theory section; the detailed supply chain description IS the theoretical argument. |

### Paper 7: Fuchs & Klann (2013) — *JIE*
**"Paying a Visit: The Dalai Lama Effect on International Trade"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Literature review → case evidence → formal hypotheses. Most "traditional" structure of all 15 papers. Theory builds from political determinants of trade → China's capacity for trade manipulation → three specific hypotheses. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "China's displeasure with countries receiving the Dalai Lama." Manipulation = bilateral meetings between the Dalai Lama and foreign leaders. |
| **Hypotheses** | 3 formal, directional hypotheses: H1 (meetings reduce exports to China), H2 (effect stronger for higher-ranked meetings), H3 (effect disappears after ~2 years). |
| **Mechanism** | Two channels: (1) government action (trade missions, customs delays), (2) consumer behavior (media → preference shifts). Sectoral decomposition (machinery/transport) supports government channel. |
| **Theory→Results** | H1 confirmed for Hu Jintao era; H2 confirmed (head-of-state > lower); H3 confirmed (2-year attenuation). Sectoral results → mechanism. |
| **Key pattern** | **"General theory → case application → formal hypotheses"** funnel. Most standard lit-review-to-hypotheses structure. |

### Paper 8: Davis, Fuchs & Johnson (2019) — *JCR*
**"State Control and the Effects of Foreign Relations on Bilateral Trade"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Clear theory section with subsections: "State Ownership as Mechanism" (3 micro-foundations: mission, personnel, financing) → "SOEs in China and India" (institutional evidence). |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "deterioration in bilateral political relations." Moderating variable = state ownership of firms (SOE vs. private). Interaction design. |
| **Hypotheses** | 1 main hypothesis (prose, not numbered): SOE imports more responsive to political tensions than private imports. |
| **Mechanism** | Three micro-foundations under one mechanism: (1) mission alignment (SOEs exist to serve state), (2) personnel dependence (political appointees), (3) financial dependence (subsidies, cheap capital). |
| **Theory→Results** | Gravity models with separate DVs for SOE/private imports + interactions. Three pathways not separately tested. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Mechanism-first with built-in placebo"**: SOE vs. private comparison is both the theoretical claim AND the identification strategy (private sector as placebo). |

### Paper 9: Dreher, Sturm & Vreeland (2009) — *JDE*
**"Development Aid and International Politics"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | "The Argument" section builds through institutional logic: UNSC structure → why votes matter → why even non-pivotal votes matter (insurance, legitimacy) → why IFIs are preferred channels (concealment, cost asymmetry). |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = manipulation = UNSC temporary membership (binary, time-limited). No separate instrument — exploits within-country variation with FE. |
| **Hypotheses** | 1 formal hypothesis (bolded): "UN Security Council membership increases the probability of receiving World Bank projects." Remarkably simple. |
| **Mechanism** | Vote-buying channeled through IFIs: UNSC seat → leverage → G7 rewards via World Bank → increased project approvals. Advantages: IFI loans more valuable than their cost, concealment from public scrutiny. |
| **Theory→Results** | Single hypothesis → project count increases ~20%. Null on loan size (consistent: easier to add small projects than increase amounts). Cold War concentration adds nuance. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Institutional logic → single clean hypothesis"**: A chain of institutional reasoning producing one precise, testable prediction. |

### Paper 10: Fisman & Miguel (2007) — *JPE*
**"Corruption, Norms, and Legal Enforcement"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Virtually no dedicated theory section. Introduction does all theoretical work: identification problem (norms vs. enforcement confounded) → design insight (diplomatic immunity removes enforcement) → predictions. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Non-standard: the "treatment" is the ABSENCE of legal enforcement (diplomatic immunity). Variation is cross-sectional (country of origin). |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses. Implicit: under zero enforcement, violations should vary with home-country corruption levels (contradicting Becker's rational crime model). |
| **Mechanism** | Cultural norms: internalized values constrain behavior even absent enforcement. The paper is deliberately agnostic about whether through personal values or social accountability. |
| **Theory→Results** | Cross-country correlation validates norms hypothesis. Enforcement reform (Clinton-Schumer) shows enforcement also matters. Both mechanisms contribute. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Design-as-theory"**: The theoretical contribution IS the insight that diplomatic immunity creates a natural experiment. Minimal formal theory needed because the design does the theoretical work. |

### Paper 11: Berger, Easterly, Nunn & Satyanath (2013) — *AER*
**"Commercial Imperialism?"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Introduction operates as a "prosecution brief": central claim → competing explanations → preview of systematic elimination. No formal model or hypotheses. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "US political influence over a foreign country." Manipulation = CIA covert interventions (binary indicator). Gap between concept and measure acknowledged. |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses. Directional predictions stated informally: US imports should increase, exports should not, effect larger in autocracies. |
| **Mechanism** | Layered: establish reduced form first (CIA intervention → US import share), then rule out alternatives (trade costs, ideology, aid), then identify specific channel: government purchases of US products. |
| **Theory→Results** | Sequential ruling-out exercise mirrors introduction structure. Autocracy/democracy heterogeneity linked to Grossman-Helpman theory. Comparative disadvantage finding is the "smoking gun." |
| **Key pattern** | **"Prosecution brief + elimination strategy"**: Introduction lays out alternatives; results systematically eliminate each, culminating in a specific mechanism. |

### Paper 12: Nunn & Qian (2014) — *AER*
**"US Food Aid and Civil Conflict"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Introduction poses research questions (not hypotheses). Background section (2.1) functions as theory: develops "aid stealing" mechanism through extensive qualitative evidence. Section 2.2 provides institutional detail motivating the instrument. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "food aid to conflict-prone countries." Manipulation = exogenous component of US food aid driven by US wheat production shocks × historical recipient propensity (shift-share). |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses. Deliberately agnostic: "the effect is a priori unclear" because multiple channels work in opposite directions. |
| **Mechanism** | Aid stealing: armed factions appropriate food during transport, using it to fund operations. Established via qualitative evidence. Heterogeneity analysis (small-scale conflicts, poor infrastructure) provides suggestive confirmation. |
| **Theory→Results** | OLS (negative, insignificant) vs. 2SLS (positive, significant) reveals endogeneity. Heterogeneity patterns connect to specific mechanism predictions. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Agnostic framing + qualitative mechanism + heterogeneity-as-evidence"**: Avoids directional hypotheses; uses heterogeneity analysis rather than formal theory to adjudicate among channels. |

### Paper 13: Autor, Dorn & Hanson (2013) — *AER*
**"The China Syndrome"**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Theory structure** | Formal model (Section I.A) derives estimating equation from monopolistic competition trade theory (Helpman-Krugman). Section I.B derives the empirical measure and instrument from model parameters. |
| **Treatment vs. manipulation** | Treatment = "rising Chinese import competition" (supply-driven). Manipulation = ΔIPWit (import exposure per worker), instrumented using Chinese imports to OTHER high-income countries. Formal model bridges concept → measure → instrument. |
| **Hypotheses** | No formal hypotheses (economics convention). Model generates structural predictions: import competition → lower wages, lower manufacturing employment, higher non-traded employment. |
| **Mechanism** | Formally modeled: China's productivity growth → export supply shocks → import competition in US → region-level employment/wage adjustments via industry specialization. |
| **Theory→Results** | Tightest theory-to-estimation pipeline: Equation (2) in model → Equation (3) in empirics. Every regression corresponds to a model parameter. |
| **Key pattern** | **"Formal model generates estimating equation"**: Unusually tight integration where the model produces both the measure and the identification strategy. |

## PART II: Systematization — How Theory Is Presented

### A. Theory Section Architecture: Five Patterns

Across the 15 papers, theory sections follow five distinct structural patterns:

| Pattern | Papers | Description | When to use |
|---------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **1. Formal model → estimating equation** | Autor et al. (2013) | Mathematical model derives the empirical measure, instrument, and predictions. | When there is established formal theory that maps to the estimand. |
| **2. Institutional chain → identification** | Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Dreher et al. (2009) | Each subsection adds one institutional link, simultaneously building the theoretical case and the identification strategy. | When the mechanism operates through institutional steps that can be traced. |
| **3. Competing hypotheses → elimination** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Berger et al. (2013) | Present 2-3 alternative explanations; structure empirics to discriminate among them. | When the phenomenon has multiple plausible explanations. |
| **4. Argument-first with hypotheses** | Malis (2021), Fuchs & Klann (2013), Dreher et al. (2018), Davis et al. (2019) | Build own argument → derive formal hypotheses → test. Closest to "standard" political science format. | When the paper proposes its own theoretical framework. |
| **5. Design-as-theory** | Fisman & Miguel (2007), Dube et al. (2013), Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), Borin et al. (2024), Nunn & Qian (2014) | The research design insight IS the theoretical contribution. Minimal or no formal theory section. | When the identification setting is so clean that the design speaks for itself, or when the claim is widely accepted and the contribution is measurement. |

**Lesson for paper_v3**: The paper's best fit is **Pattern 4** (argument-first with hypotheses), combined with elements of **Pattern 3** (competing hypotheses — Spec 1 vs. Spec 2, attenuation as discrimination). The current structure of paper_v3 leans too much toward Pattern 5 (letting the design speak) without having a design as clean as Fisman & Miguel's.

---

### B. Treatment vs. Manipulation Distinction

| Distinction type | Papers | How handled |
|-----------------|--------|-------------|
| **Explicit conceptual/formal distinction** | Autor et al. (2013) | Formal model bridges "China supply shocks" (concept) to ΔIPWit (measure) to instrument (other countries' imports). |
| **Implicit but clear** | Malis (2021), Dube et al. (2013), Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Berger et al. (2013), Nunn & Qian (2014) | The theoretical concept and the empirical lever are clearly different things, connected through institutional or behavioral logic. |
| **Treatment ≈ manipulation** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Dreher et al. (2009), Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003) | The empirical variable IS the theorized treatment (UNSC membership, terrorism). Identification comes from timing/institutional features, not from separating concept from measure. |
| **Moderating variable design** | Davis et al. (2019) | The "treatment" is political tensions; the theoretical claim is about the *moderator* (SOE vs. private). The moderator is the mechanism. |
| **Blurred/bundled** | Flores-Macias & Kreps (2013), Borin et al. (2024) | Multiple treatments bundled; concept-to-measure gap acknowledged but not resolved. |

**Lesson for paper_v3**: The paper should be in the "implicit but clear" category. The treatment (status/prestige) and the manipulation (rank reversal) are conceptually distinct but connected through coarse categorization theory. The consolidated-revision-guide already recommends distinguishing "trade status" (ordinal position) from "status shock" (the rank-reversal event). This maps well to the Malis model, where "diplomatic representation quality" (treatment) is distinct from "ambassadorial rotation" (manipulation).

### C. Hypothesis Formulation Patterns

| Hypothesis format | Papers | Convention |
|-------------------|--------|------------|
| **Formal, numbered (H1, H2...)** | Malis (2021), Fuchs & Klann (2013), Dreher et al. (2018) | Standard in political science journals (IO, ISQ, JIE). |
| **Single hypothesis, bolded/prominent** | Dreher et al. (2009) | Common when the paper has one clean prediction. |
| **Prose expectations, not labeled** | Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Kuziemko & Werker (2006) | Common in AJPS and economics journals. |
| **No hypotheses; model generates predictions** | Autor et al. (2013) | Standard in economics when a formal model is present. |
| **No hypotheses; design-driven** | Fisman & Miguel (2007), Dube et al. (2013), Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), Borin et al. (2024) | When the contribution is measurement/design, not theory testing. |
| **Agnostic framing (direction unclear)** | Nunn & Qian (2014) | When multiple channels predict opposite signs; paper estimates net effect. |

**Key finding**: Economics journals (AER, JPE, JDE) never use formal numbered hypotheses. Political science journals (IO, AJPS, ISQ, JCR, APSR) sometimes do, sometimes don't. The choice depends more on the paper's theoretical ambition than on the journal.

**Lesson for paper_v3**: Given the target journals (IO, ISQ) and the need to strengthen the theoretical contribution, formal numbered hypotheses (H1-H4) are appropriate and recommended, following the Dreher et al. (2018) and Malis (2021) models.

---

### D. Hypothesis Level: Treatment vs. Manipulation

| Level | Papers | Justification |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| **Manipulation level** | Fuchs & Klann (2013), Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Dreher et al. (2009), Dube et al. (2013) | Hypotheses are about what is actually tested: Dalai Lama meetings, UNSC membership, FAWB expiration. |
| **Treatment level** | Malis (2021), Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Dreher et al. (2018) | Hypotheses are about the broader concept: diplomatic representation quality, aid conditionality, foreign policy interests. |
| **Structural (model-derived)** | Autor et al. (2013) | Predictions are about model parameters, not about a single treatment or manipulation. |
| **Mixed** | Davis et al. (2019), Berger et al. (2013) | Some predictions at the treatment level, some at the manipulation level. |

**Key finding**: The most convincing papers match hypothesis level to what the empirical design actually identifies. When there is a gap between treatment and manipulation (as in Malis), the paper explicitly builds the bridge through theory (agency theory explains WHY ambassadorial rotation matters for diplomatic quality).

**Lesson for paper_v3**: Hypotheses should be at the **manipulation level** (rank reversal), with the broader concept (status) providing the theoretical motivation. This matches the Edmans (2025) criteria: precision, strength, correspondence to what is tested. The bridge between "status" and "rank reversal" is provided by coarse categorization theory.

### E. Mechanism Presentation

| Pattern | Papers | Description |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| **Single mechanism, deeply developed** | Malis (2021), Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Dreher et al. (2009) | One mechanism traced through multiple steps with institutional detail. |
| **Multiple complementary channels** | Davis et al. (2019), Fuchs & Klann (2013) | 2-3 channels presented as complementary (mission/personnel/financing; government/consumer). Design cannot always distinguish them. |
| **Competing mechanisms for elimination** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Berger et al. (2013) | Multiple mechanisms presented as alternatives; evidence discriminates. |
| **Mechanism through heterogeneity** | Nunn & Qian (2014), Autor et al. (2013) | Reduced-form effect established first; mechanism evidence from heterogeneity analysis. |
| **Mechanism unobserved / acknowledged gap** | Flores-Macias & Kreps (2013) | Mechanism theorized but not directly observed; paper measures reduced form only. |
| **Mechanism irrelevant / not attempted** | Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), Borin et al. (2024) | Paper measures total effect without decomposing into channels. |

**Key finding**: The strongest papers are explicit about which channels their design can and cannot distinguish. Kuziemko & Werker (2006) explicitly acknowledges it cannot observe vote-trading negotiations directly. Carnegie & Marinov uses qualitative evidence on Commission behavior to support the conditionality channel. Davis et al. (2019) acknowledges the three SOE micro-foundations are not separately testable.

**Lesson for paper_v3**: The paper should present complementary channels (cognitive, media, coordination) following the Davis et al. model, while being explicit that the SDiD estimates the **combined reduced-form effect** and the NLP evidence provides **suggestive evidence for one specific channel** (media/attention). This is exactly what the consolidated-revision-guide recommends.

## PART III: Systematization — Theory→Results Connection

### A. Connection Architectures

| Architecture | Papers | How it works |
|--------------|--------|-------------|
| **Direct derivation** | Autor et al. (2013) | Theory model equation → estimating equation. Tightest possible link. |
| **Hypothesis testing** | Malis (2021), Fuchs & Klann (2013), Dreher et al. (2018), Dreher et al. (2009) | Formal hypotheses → specific regressions/tables test each one. |
| **Elimination strategy** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Berger et al. (2013) | Alternative explanations → each empirically eliminated → remaining explanation supported. |
| **Temporal signature matching** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006), Fuchs & Klann (2013), Carnegie & Marinov (2017) | Theory predicts specific temporal pattern; event study or dynamic analysis confirms/rejects. |
| **Heterogeneity as mechanism evidence** | Davis et al. (2019), Nunn & Qian (2014), Berger et al. (2013) | Main effect established; then subgroup/interaction analysis tests mechanism predictions. |
| **Placebo/falsification** | Malis (2021), Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), Dube et al. (2013) | Test prediction of null effect where theory says there should be none. |

**Lesson for paper_v3**: The paper already has elements of several architectures. The most underexploited is **temporal signature matching** — the dynamic event study attenuation is powerful discriminating evidence (salience predicts transience; structural bandwagoning predicts persistence). The **Spec 1 vs. Spec 2 comparison** is a form of heterogeneity-as-mechanism (hegemon displacement as moderator). Both need to be explicitly connected to hypotheses.

### B. How Papers Handle What They Can and Cannot Identify

| Approach | Papers | Language used |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| **Explicit about the gap** | Kuziemko & Werker (2006) | "Cannot directly observe vote-trading negotiations" |
| **"Consistent with" language** | Carnegie & Marinov (2017), Nunn & Qian (2014) | "Consistent with the conditionality mechanism," "suggestive evidence" |
| **Channels not separately tested** | Davis et al. (2019), Fuchs & Klann (2013) | "Cannot distinguish mission from personnel from financing channels" |
| **Net effect, not decomposed** | Nunn & Qian (2014), Autor et al. (2013) | "Average causal effect," "net of positive and negative channels" |
| **Overclaims** | Flores-Macias & Kreps (2013) | "First systematic evidence" — contested instrument |

**Lesson for paper_v3**: The paper should follow the Carnegie & Marinov / Davis et al. model: be explicit that the SDiD identifies the **combined reduced-form effect** (all channels together), while the NLP evidence provides **"suggestive" / "consistent with"** evidence for the attention channel specifically. This is the "structured hybrid" framing recommended in all review documents.

### C. Role of Scope Conditions

| Approach | Papers | How presented |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Theoretical scope conditions before results** | Malis (2021) | "Continuity during turnover" subsection explicitly bounds predictions. |
| **Scope conditions as empirical design** | Davis et al. (2019) | SOE vs. private distinction IS a scope condition tested as an interaction. |
| **Scope conditions discussed in results** | Fuchs & Klann (2013) | Hu Jintao era vs. earlier (scope condition discovered empirically). |
| **Minimal explicit discussion** | Most economics papers | Scope conditions implied by the setting, not explicitly theorized. |

**Lesson for paper_v3**: Political science journals expect explicit scope conditions (Malis, Dreher et al. 2018). The paper should present scope conditions in prose after the hypotheses: (1) hegemon displacement, (2) free press, (3) entrenched prior relationship, (4) democratic accountability, (5) exogeneity of rank reversal. Some of these are tested (H2, H5, H6 in the consolidated guide) and some acknowledged as limitations.
