# Mechanism Evidence: China as Brazil's Top Trading Partner, 2009-2011

## Summary

The search found direct, verified evidence that Brazilian political and diplomatic actors used rank/status language about China as Brazil's top trading partner during 2009-2011. The strongest evidence comes from official MRE/FUNAG `Resenha de Política Exterior do Brasil` volumes and one official Chamber of Deputies item. The evidence is not merely that trade grew: several documents explicitly connect China's new commercial rank to diplomatic visits, the Brazil-China Strategic Partnership, the 2010-2014 Joint Action Plan, trade/investment diversification, and, in October 2011, an internal Itamaraty China task force.

The evidence supports a careful version of the paper's mechanism: the 2009 overtaking generated a public and official rank cue that Brazilian leaders could invoke to justify deeper, selective accommodation and institutional attention to China. The strongest sources do not prove that the rank cue alone caused foreign-policy change; they show that officials used the rank cue when framing and legitimating the bilateral agenda.

## Assessment

The evidence is strongest for the claim that Brazilian officials publicly recognized China's rank and used that recognition in diplomatic and institutional contexts. Lula used the rank cue during his May 2009 China visit while discussing the strategic partnership, trade diversification, investment, presidential diplomacy, and the future Joint Action Plan. MRE then reused the cue in 2010 and 2011 when framing Hu Jintao's visit, Dilma Rousseff's state visit, the joint communique, and Chen Deming's trade mission. Patriota's October 2011 speech is especially useful because it moves beyond ceremonial language: he identified China as the focus of a dedicated Itamaraty task force created to monitor economic-commercial relations and propose ways to expand and diversify them.

The main limitation is causal leverage. The sources show direct mechanism language and policy framing, but they do not by themselves establish that officials changed positions only because China became the top partner. The most defensible formulation is therefore that the rank shift became a salient official justification and coordination cue, not that it mechanically determined all subsequent accommodation.

## Direct Evidence vs. Contextual Evidence

Direct evidence includes MECH002-MECH005, MECH008-MECH009, and MECH011: these sources use explicit top-partner language and link it to visits, strategic partnership language, the PAC, diversification, reciprocal investment, or Itamaraty institutional action. Contextual evidence includes MECH001, which shows the rise of status language before the full 2009 overtaking, and MECH010, which shows the same cue used in a legislative setting to support a civil/commercial judicial-cooperation agreement.

## Gaps

The local UNGA/AGNU material did not provide direct evidence for the commercial-rank mechanism. The Fala.BR/CGU scan found relevant LAI requests and evidence that MRE holds or held China-related telegrams and mission reports, but the public text available in the filtered open-data zips did not yield verified 2009-2011 telegram excerpts with the mechanism language. Some LAI items point to promising future requests, especially the 2011 Dilma China visit telegram package and mission reports for Beijing/Shanghai.

## Block for Paper

Contemporary primary sources show that Brazilian officials themselves used China's new commercial rank as a public cue for diplomatic prioritization. In a written interview before his May 2009 China visit, President Lula described China as Brazil's top trading partner while explaining that the visit would reinforce a partnership organized around commerce, technology, and global-governance coordination. Three days later in Beijing, Lula repeated that China had become Brazil's largest or principal commercial partner and embedded that claim in speeches about the strategic partnership, trade diversification, investment, presidential diplomacy, and the decision to elaborate the 2010-2014 Brazil-China Joint Action Plan.

The rank cue then became part of official policy language. MRE's April 2010 note on Hu Jintao's state visit invoked China's 2009 position as Brazil's largest trading partner while announcing the deepening of the Strategic Partnership and the signature of the Joint Action Plan. The April 2011 Dilma-Hu joint communique stated that China had become Brazil's principal commercial partner in 2009 and immediately tied that status to intensified dialogue on trade and investment structures, trade diversification, reciprocal investment, and implementation of the PAC. In October 2011, Foreign Minister Antonio Patriota went further: when discussing China, he announced an Itamaraty task force to monitor relations with Brazil's principal commercial partner and to propose ways to expand and diversify the relationship beyond commodity complementarity.

These documents do not show that the rank shift mechanically caused every subsequent foreign-policy adjustment. They do show that the 2009 overtaking was converted into an official, publicly usable status cue, one that Brazilian leaders used to justify deeper diplomatic engagement and institutional attention to China.

## Short Footnote or Appendix Version

Official MRE/FUNAG documents show the mechanism directly. During Lula's May 2009 China visit, the president used explicit top-partner language while discussing the strategic partnership, trade diversification, presidential diplomacy, and the future 2010-2014 Joint Action Plan. MRE repeated the same cue in notes on Hu Jintao's 2010 visit and Dilma Rousseff's 2011 state visit, and the 2011 Dilma-Hu communique linked China's 2009 rank to intensified trade/investment dialogue and diversification. Patriota's October 2011 speech provides the clearest institutional evidence: he described China as Brazil's principal commercial partner while announcing an Itamaraty task force to monitor and expand the relationship.
