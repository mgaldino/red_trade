## Overall Feedback

Here are some overall reactions to the document.

**Outline**

The paper has an original idea and does more diagnostic work than many single-case SDiD papers, but the design still does not cleanly identify a discrete status cue as distinct from smoother trade growth and Brazil's broader foreign-policy reorientation. The Brazil result is suggestive; the broader panel and mechanism sections are not yet strong enough to support the paper's larger general claims.

The paper's best feature is that it is unusually explicit about its estimand and about several of its own limits in Sections 1, 3.1, 5, and 7. The Brazil case is substantively interesting, the diagnostic tables are informative, and the attempt to combine reduced-form evidence with media and vote-level material is thoughtful. The main weakness is that the headline contribution is about a threshold-based status mechanism, and that is the part the current evidence distinguishes least well from alternative stories.

**Theory and empirical tests are misaligned on scope conditions**

Section 2 says the mechanism should be strongest when the cue is publicly recognized and when China displaces a politically prominent incumbent, ideally the United States. The Brazil case fits that theory, but the cross-country test in Section 6 pools very different treated countries, many without demonstrated public salience and with displaced incumbents that are not comparable to the US. Section 3 and Table 20 already acknowledge that recoverability of public cues is weak outside Brazil and that metric mismatch is real in cases such as Australia. That means the panel is not testing the same treatment the theory defines; it is testing a noisier goods-export rank event that may or may not have been politically legible. This matters because the paper uses the panel to move from an illuminating case to a broader claim about trade-based status gains. A revision should either narrow the theory to what the panel actually measures or restrict the panel to cases where public salience and incumbent displacement are validated in local sources.

**The paper does not yet show that rank 1 matters beyond smooth trade growth**

The Introduction and Section 2 frame the contribution as showing that a discrete rank reversal matters over and above continuous trade growth. Yet the empirical evidence never cleanly demonstrates a break at the threshold itself. Table 4 offers pseudo-onsets, but they are presented only as point estimates without placebo inference, and the 2004 rank-2 estimate is already meaningfully negative relative to zero. Figure 4 is a useful donor diagnostic, but by construction it only studies below-threshold donors and therefore cannot establish that rank 1 has a distinct effect relative to near-rank exposure. The paper also admits that actors could observe the trade trend before 2009, which makes anticipation a first-order concern rather than a side issue. To support the status-threshold claim, the revision needs formal lead tests, comparisons to rank-2 or near-miss cases, and a design that shows the threshold adds something beyond smooth China exposure.

**The Brazil SDiD result rests on narrow support and imperfect control comparability**

Sections 3.1 and 4 present the Brazil SDiD as the main causal evidence, but the identifying support is thinner than the draft sometimes implies. Table 10 shows heavy time-weight concentration in 2007 and 2008, exactly the years closest to treatment and the years most entangled with the financial crisis and Brazil's changing external posture. Table 16 also shows that several high-weight donors were themselves strongly exposed to China's rise, including one case where China was already the top export destination and others where it was top two. Good pre-fit and diffuse unit weights help, but they do not settle whether the donor pool provides a credible untreated counterfactual for a China-rise treatment. The placebo ranks in Table 13 are also coarse and depend on donor comparability, which the paper itself notes but still uses heavily in the headline result. The fix is to rebuild the SDiD credibility section around cleaner donor pools, explicit exclusion or stratification by China exposure, and sensitivity to the late-preperiod leverage built into the time weights.

**Brazil-specific confounding in 2009 remains a live alternative explanation**

The paper works hard to address the obvious 2009 confounds, but the Brazil result still sits in a crowded historical moment. Sections 3.1 and 4 discuss the commodity cycle, the global financial crisis, the BRICS and G20 moment, and Lula-era South-South diplomacy, yet most of the evidence against these alternatives is indirect. Table 5 shows stability to several interaction terms, but the paper rightly admits that these specifications include the first treated year and can absorb part of the mechanism, so they are not strong exclusion tests. The cross-country panel helps with the narrow claim that Brazil's calendar year alone is special, but it does not rule out a broader explanation in which countries that cross the China-top threshold are precisely those most exposed to the same China-rise shocks. This matters because the current draft sometimes reads as if timing checks have largely closed the confounding problem, when they have only narrowed it. The revision should treat Brazil-specific political and global-demand alternatives as live rivals and test them more directly, through placebo outcomes, rival timing benchmarks, or process evidence tied to UNGA positioning rather than bilateral diplomacy alone.

**The outcome and mechanism evidence do not isolate selective movement toward China**

The outcome and mechanism sections do not yet show clearly enough that the paper is capturing selective movement toward China, rather than a broader shift away from the United States or a change in the agenda being voted on. The main outcome in Section 3 is absolute ideal-point distance to China, which is a broad summary measure and can move for several reasons that are not identical to China-specific accommodation. Section 4.1 partly addresses this with China-US divergent votes and a triple-difference design, but those results are explicitly described as non-causal diagnostics and rely on a short pre-period. Section 5 then shows trade-topic salience and official uptake of the rank cue, but it does not link those signals tightly to the specific vote domains where the paper claims the effect is strongest. As written, the mechanism evidence shows that the cue existed and was used in rhetoric; it does not yet show that the cue did causal work in UNGA behavior. A stronger revision would center the China-versus-US disagreement outcome more directly, add robustness to alternative vote subsets or alignment measures, and narrow the mechanism claim unless cue invocation can be connected to the observed vote shifts.

**The cross-country extension is too fragile to carry the paper's broader claims**

The cross-country extension in Section 6 is too fragile to carry much of the paper's general contribution in its current form. The main IFE estimate is directionally supportive, but Appendix Tables 27 and 28 show that it becomes small and statistically weak under the alternative UNGA ideal-point measure and is sensitive to the number of latent factors and the time window. Section 3 already warns that treatment coding outside Brazil contains measurement error because public language often tracks broader trade metrics rather than goods-export rank. Taken together, that means the panel evidence is better read as exploratory pattern matching than as a strong second pillar of the argument. This matters because the Introduction and Conclusion use the panel to argue that the Brazil finding extends beyond one revealing case. The revision should either strengthen the panel substantially or recast it as a limited scope probe whose main job is to show that Brazil is not the only case moving in the predicted direction.

**No simulation showing threshold designs can separate DGPs**

The paper asks readers to trust that its SDiD and IFE procedures can tell a discrete rank-entry effect from a smooth China-rise process, but it never shows that in a setting where the truth is known. That omission matters because the headline contribution is exactly that the threshold adds something beyond continuous exposure, and the current evidence comes only from the realized sample. A calibrated placebo Monte Carlo would do real work here. Build one DGP on the 1997-2015 Brazil panel where UNGA distance responds only to smooth growth in China exposure, and a second DGP that adds a one-time drop at the 2009 rank crossing while preserving the same pre-treatment variance, donor exposure, and post-2009 shock structure. Then run the paper's SDiD, placebo-rank inference, timing falsifications, and the cross-country IFE code on both DGPs and report false-positive rates, power, and how often the procedures recover the threshold story.

**No worked model of the justification mechanism**

The theory says public rank recognition lowers the political cost of moving toward China, especially when the United States is the displaced incumbent and when the vote domain is visible but not too costly. Yet the manuscript never writes down a simple model where those ingredients generate the paper's own predictions. Without that step, readers cannot see why human-rights votes should move more than other votes, why durability should matter, or how the status account nests the familiar continuous-interdependence story. A short spatial foreign-policy model with audience costs would fix this. Let the government choose a UNGA position between the US and China, with payoffs from trade exposure, domestic political cost, and a justificatory cost that falls discretely once China becomes the top export destination and the cue is public. Compute the comparative statics for three cases: no public cue, public cue without US displacement, and public cue with US displacement; then show that the existing continuous-exposure account is the special case where the discrete cost drop is zero.

**Durability claim lacks a transient-spell benchmark**

The paper makes durability part of the theory, not just part of the coding rule: becoming number one is supposed to matter because the label can be reused over time. But the draft never shows a transient China-top episode side by side with a durable one, so the five-year rule still reads like a sensible filter rather than a demonstrated implication. This matters because "becomes and remains there" sits in the title, abstract, and treatment definition. A direct benchmark is missing. Use the 26 short China-top episodes excluded in Table 19 to estimate a separate short-spell event study, or fit the same IFE design separately for 1-2 year, 3-4 year, and 5+ year spells. If only the durable spells show sustained post-entry movement, the paper would finally show that durability is doing theoretical work rather than serving as a researcher's screen.

**Public-cue concept lacks a reusable coding protocol**

The appendix gives examples and caveats, but it still does not supply a reusable rule for coding the paper's core concept: a publicly legible trade-status cue. That gap matters because the manuscript wants to offer a broader way to study economic influence, and readers need to know how to identify the treatment outside Brazil without falling back on a rough goods-only proxy. Right now the paper shows that measurement can be tricky, not how to do it well. Add a short coding appendix that fixes the search window around entry, lists the source types that count, defines the exact rank-language patterns that qualify, records the displaced incumbent, and flags whether the wording tracks goods exports, total exports, or aggregate trade. Then apply that rule to a small validation sample drawn from Table 23, such as Brazil, Chile, Peru, Australia, and South Africa, and report simple recovery and mismatch rates. That would make the concept usable for other cases and would tell readers when the cross-country proxy can and cannot carry the paper's claims.

**Recommendation**: major revision: The paper has a publishable idea and a fair amount of disciplined empirical work, but the present draft does not yet separate a discrete status-threshold effect from continuous trade growth, Brazil-specific shocks, and broader China-rise dynamics. For a top venue, the cross-country extension and mechanism evidence also need a narrower interpretation or stronger validation, because they currently do less than the introduction and conclusion suggest.

**Key revision targets**:

1. Show more directly that rank 1 matters beyond smooth growth by adding formal lead and anticipation tests, inferential timing falsifications, and comparisons to rank-2 or near-miss cases.
2. Rebuild the Brazil counterfactual credibility section around cleaner support by addressing China-exposed donors, reporting sensitivity to excluding contaminated donors, and clarifying the limits of placebo-rank inference with one treated unit.
3. Align theory and evidence on scope conditions by deciding whether the theory is about any China top-export entry or only publicly salient cases that displace a hegemonic incumbent, then subset or recode the cross-country panel accordingly.
4. Make China-specific selective alignment a central empirical target by elevating China-US divergent vote analyses, strengthening issue-area identification, and narrowing claims if aggregate ideal-point distance remains the main outcome.
5. Treat the cross-country panel and media evidence more conservatively unless stronger validation is added, including direct salience coding across countries, explicit rank-language counts, and robustness across both ideal-point measures and factor choices.

## Detailed Comments (4)

### 1. SCM Display Does Not Match the Estimator

**Status**: [Pending]

**Quote**:
> Here 𝑤̂ 𝑖SCM is the donor-unit weight assigned to unit 𝑖. SCM uses these donor weights
> to match the treated unit’s pre-treatment outcome path, and effects are then read from
> post-treatment treated-minus-synthetic gaps.

**Feedback**:
The sentence under the display describes standard synthetic control, but the displayed objective above it does not. As written, the optimization puts the treatment-effect term inside a weighted least-squares problem over all units and all periods, which uses post-treatment outcomes in the fitting step. That is not the estimator this paragraph is explaining. Replace the display with the standard pre-treatment weight-choice problem, with nonnegative donor weights summing to one, and then state separately that effects are read from post-treatment treated-minus-synthetic gaps.

### 2. Australia’s Entry Year Is Coded Two Ways

**Status**: [Pending]

**Quote**:
> Australia illustrates the point. The Australian
> Financial Review reported in 2009 that China was Australia’s largest aggregate trading
> partner, combining exports and imports of goods and services, before Australia meets
> the paper’s stricter goods-export-destination treatment criterion.

**Feedback**:
The Australia example needs one entry year, and the paper currently gives two. This paragraph says the 2009 AFR story came before Australia met the strict goods-export criterion. Table 20 assigns Australia an entry year of 2010. Table 23 then codes the treatment year as 2009 and reports 14 treated years in a 1990–2022 panel, which matches 2009, not 2010. Align the narrative and both tables to one coding rule. If 2010 is the intended entry, the treated and untreated counts in Table 23 need to change as well.

### 3. Latin America Placebo Replications Are Reported Inconsistently

**Status**: [Pending]

**Quote**:
> Placebo-based SEs are in parentheses (20,000 replications for the preferred column; 5,000 for the comparison columns).

**Feedback**:
The replication count for the Latin America no-covariate estimate is recorded two different ways. Table 3 says comparison columns use 5,000 placebo replications, which includes column (5). Figure 11 and the paragraph below it report the same ATT and SE for that estimate but say the SE uses 20,000 replications. The result itself does not change, but the Monte Carlo setting should be stated once and stated the same way everywhere so the estimate is reproducible.

### 4. Brazil Metric Equivalence Is Stated Too Strongly

**Status**: [Pending]

**Quote**:
> In Brazil, public and official language tracks that rank: the 2009 coverage announcing
> that China had overtaken the United States as the country’s largest trading partner was
> reporting precisely that change.

**Feedback**:
The Brazil case may still be the closest metric match in the paper, but this sentence claims more than the text has shown. Section 5 supports the point with broader “largest trading partner” language and then cites export-purchase figures for China and the United States, not total bilateral trade. That is enough to support a close proxy claim. It is not enough to say the coverage was “reporting precisely that change.” Soften the sentence and present the Brazil wording as closely tracking the export-destination reversal rather than as an exact verbal match.
