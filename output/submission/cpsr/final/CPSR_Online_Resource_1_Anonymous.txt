Triple-Difference Pre-Trends
Figure S2 reports the input to the identification assumption behind the triple difference.
Each series is Brazil's average distance to China minus its distance to the United States,
net of the donor-pool average in the same year, computed separately for human-rights votes
and for every other vote where China and the United States voted differently. What the
triple difference requires is that these two series move together before treatment, so that any
common drift cancels when one domain is differenced against the other.

LetN 0 andN 1 denote the numbers of control and treated units, and letT 0 andT 1 denote the
numbers of pre- and post-treatment periods. Classic DiD corresponds to uniform weighting
in this comparison. SDiD instead learns unit weights from pre-treatment outcome histories
and assigns optimized weights to pre-treatment periods so that their weighted control-unit

the full SDiD time-weight vector, optimized nonnegative pre-treatment weights sum to one,

to estimate the effect precisely. The point estimate is nevertheless consistent with the main
result and, if anything, is somewhat larger in absolute value than the estimate from the larger
global donor pool.

Selected Public-Cue Cross-Country Analyses
This section reports the diagnostics underlying the proposed replacement for the cross-
country section. The common country-year window is 1997-2022, and the outcome is absolute
UNGA ideal-point distance to China. The individual SDiD models use 105 countries that
never have China as their largest goods-export destination during this window. Each indi-
vidual estimate uses a placebo standard error from 5,000 re-estimations. The pooled models
use the interactive fixed-effects estimator infect2.4.5, with the number of latent factors
selected by cross-validation and 1,000 unit-bootstrap replications.
Figure S5 compares the focal cases before their selected public-cue years. Gabon has the
smallest mean pre-cue distance to China among the five cases (0.182), which motivates treat-
ing its limited room for further convergence as a scope consideration rather than interpreting
its exclusion as evidence that no cue occurred.

Public Cue and Recoverability Audit
To assess how closely the empirical treatment used in the panel regression - China becoming
a country's largest destination for goods exports - maps onto the theorized treatment of a
publicly salient status change, I use a source audit originally constructed for 14 countries in
an earlier no-covariate treated sample. This audit is not a census of the 35 countries treated
in the current goods-only restricted-risk-set specification: 13 audited countries remain in the
current treated sample, Qatar does not, and 22 current treated countries were not included
in the source audit. The underlying matrix records both recovered and unrecovered China
cues. Table S14 displays the nine cases in which the China cue is currently coded as unknown;
Chile, Brazil, Uruguay, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar have recovered cues and are omitted by the
display filter.
The table distinguishes weak observation from cases in which public rank language about
the displaced incumbent is recoverable. For the China cue,mediummeans one countable
strong or moderate first-window source with explicit rank language. For the former-incumbent
benchmark,mediummeans either one countable source with explicit incumbent-rank or dis-
placement language, or at least two countable trade-coverage records from at least one
independent source without a clear top-rank label. A countable source must be eligible,
fall within one year of the source-audit entry year, be coded strong or moderate, enter the
benchmark, and not be markedDO_NOT_COUNT.Unknownmeans that the incumbent was not
identified or that no countable benchmark source was recovered; no case is currently coded
low. Non-recovery is not evidence that a cue was absent because contemporaneous local or
official records may be inaccessible or inconsistent with the trade-rank series. Except for Aus-
tralia, whose displayed year has been reconciled to the current 2009 entry, the table retains
the entry windows used in the legacy source audit. Table S15 reports Australia's broad-trade
source separately.

Measurement Robustness: UNGA-DM Ideal Points
The main estimations in the paper used the classic ideal-point measurement by BSV (Bailey
et al. 2017) as the response variable. However, according to Fjelstul et al. (2026), the set of
resolutions included in the BSV dataset is not complete. They provided a broader dataset,
along with re-estimations of ideal points based on the new data. To assess whether the results
of the paper change with this new measurement, I reran the main estimations with this new
response variable. To make the comparison as close as possible, I held the treatment definition,
donor and risk-set rules, covariate structure, and inference conventions fixed. The UNGA-DM
series has a shorter time window, and the SDiD unit and time weights and the IFE factor
count are re-estimated by their algorithms in response to the outcome series. These induced
adjustments mean that the comparison evaluates the robustness of the analysis package to
the alternative outcome; it is not a pure measurement-only perturbation of an otherwise
identical fitted counterfactual. It is also important to note that 28 country-session rows had
no ISO3c match and 0 SDiD rows had no outcome in the alternative measurement.
The two outcome measures preserve the negative direction of the Brazilian SDiD estimate,
but they do not support a blanket claim of robustness across the Brazilian and cross-country
designs. Table S18 shows the comparison. Under UNGA-DM, the point estimate is nominally
higher than the original in absolute terms (-0.331 versus -0.273), with a lower p-value of 0.008.
The directional rank is the same in both measurements (3/96 (0.031)). The bilateral rank
improves from 7/96 (0.073) to 5/96 (0.052). The pre-treatment means differ (0.647 under
BSV versus 0.837 under UNGA-DM). But the effect measured as a percentage of the pre-
treatment average is similar: -42.2 and -39.5 percent. The Brazilian result therefore retains
its direction and inferential support under the alternative measure, with a similar relative
magnitude. The cross-country comparison below is more sensitive to the outcome source:
its IFE point estimate is closer to zero on the UNGA-DM scale and its uncertainty interval
includes zero. The2×2comparison below holds the common window and country-year panel
fixed while varying the outcome source and factor count, allowing conditional factor-count
comparisons within each measure. It does not provide a unique, scale-invariant decomposition
of the difference between outcomes.
